Store analytics notes
Most shoppers ask about delivery dates; few ask about the returns window.
The buyer is often also the recipient.
Basket size tends to rise with product views, though neither causes the other.
A sale's source is the first ad the shopper clicked.
Orders and invoices match one to one, and a split order has more than one parcel.
Products join to suppliers many to many.
The join from orders to invoices is certified.
Some customers are wholesale buyers.
A customer's region is the last region on their address history.
Wholesale buyers usually order by phone.
Sales are often looked at by region and by device.
Returns are sometimes divided by reason into damaged and unwanted.
Shoppers are sometimes called guests, especially when they have no account.
Order data is missing from 1 to 3 June 2026.
Q1 sales were restated on 10 May 2026 to $1.2 million.
Orders over the last 7 days are counted every day.
Sales year to date add up from the start of the fiscal year, on the fiscal calendar.
Sales the same week last year are compared with this week's sales.
Cart conversion is the share of carts that become orders within 2 days.
Stock is the last count of each day for each warehouse, and is never summed across days.
Days with no sales show 0.
Store managers see only the orders of their own store.
A viewer's store is the store_id on their staff record.
Whoever requests a refund may not approve it.
No customer gets more than 3 refunds per calendar month.
A refund over $500 needs approval from two different managers.
For the sales dashboard, Finance is consulted and the store managers are kept informed.
To find customers with no order, keep the customers that have no match in orders.
Orders join to customers once as the buyer and once as the recipient.
Customers are identified by customer_id, and also by email, which can change.
A shop customer and a newsletter contact count as one customer when their trimmed, lowercased emails match.
Privacy rules override the store guidelines.
Orders should match payments one to one, but split payments make duplicates.
Only a metric's owner may change its definition.
Delivery time is the 95th percentile of hours to deliver, always one of the actual times.
The newsletter keeps customer numbers as text; compare them with the shop's customer_id as numbers.
Warehouse postcodes are compared with order postcodes as text.
The "Order Items" table in the "Sales" schema has the columns "Order Date", "orderId" and "OrderID".
Some shoppers pay by invoice.
